How to Start a Yoga Practice With a Friend

Published 2026-02-10 · Desert Roses Yoga Dance

Practicing with a friend improves consistency mainly through accountability — you're less likely to skip a class you've already told someone you'll be at — and it lowers the anxiety of a first class, since you're not walking into an unfamiliar room alone.

The habit problem yoga alone doesn't solve

New yoga habits fail for a boring reason: it's easy to cancel plans you've only made with yourself. A scheduled class with a friend changes the social cost of skipping — you're not just breaking a promise to your own calendar, you're leaving someone standing at the studio door.

It also solves the 'first class' problem

The single biggest reason people put off trying yoga isn't time or money — it's walking into a room of strangers not knowing what to do with their body. Bringing a friend to your very first class removes that entirely. You already have someone to catch your eye during an unfamiliar pose, or to laugh with when neither of you can hold tree pose yet.

Making the invite easy

The lowest-friction way to bring someone new is to invite them to a first class that's already free, so the ask isn't 'commit to a package with me' — it's just 'come try one class, no cost, no pressure.' That's exactly how Desert Roses structures new-student trials at both the Chinatown and Paya Lebar studios.

Keep the accountability going after the first class

  • Book the same recurring time slot together each week, rather than deciding fresh each time.
  • Pick one class format both of you are curious about — Mandarin Yoga and Yogalates are both approachable starting points.
  • Treat a missed class as a reschedule, not a dropped habit — message each other and rebook.
