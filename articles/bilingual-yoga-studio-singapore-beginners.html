Bilingual Yoga Studio in Singapore: A Beginner's Guide

Desert Roses Yoga Dance · Published August 20, 2026

Desert Roses Yoga Dance teaches yoga in both English and Mandarin at its Chinatown and Paya Lebar studios in Singapore, so beginners can learn in whichever language they're most comfortable with — and switch between the two as they progress.

Singapore's yoga scene is full of studios, but very few genuinely teach in two languages side by side. Desert Roses Yoga Dance has run bilingual classes — English and Mandarin — since 2009, which means a first-time student can choose the language that feels most natural, whether that's their working language or their mother tongue.

Why bilingual instruction matters for beginners

Yoga terminology can be confusing enough in one language. Instructions like "externally rotate your front hip" or "soften your gaze" are precise, and precision matters for safety and progress. A bilingual class means instructors can clarify a cue in whichever language lands — useful for students who grew up speaking Mandarin at home but work in English, or vice versa, and for students bringing a parent or grandparent who is more comfortable in Mandarin.

What to expect at your first class

A first Mandarin Yoga or general yoga class at Desert Roses typically runs through a warm-up, a sequence of standing and seated postures, and a short seated or lying-down closing practice. Instructors walk the room individually, which matters more for beginners than any single posture — most first-class nervousness is really about not knowing whether you're doing it "right," and hands-on, bilingual correction solves that quickly.

  • Wear comfortable, stretchy clothing you can move in.
  • Arrive 10 minutes early for your first class to fill in a short form.
  • Bring a water bottle — mats are provided at the studio.
  • No experience or flexibility is required to start.

Your first class is free

Desert Roses offers a free first class to every new student, with no obligation to purchase a package afterward. After the trial, a Yoga package is 99 SGD for 10 classes valid over 3 months, or a Yoga & Dance Combo package is 98 SGD for 8 classes valid over 2 months — both designed for beginners who want to try more than one class type before committing.

Two studios, one community

Classes run at two Singapore locations: 22B Upper Cross Street in Chinatown, and 706A Geylang Road in Paya Lebar. Both are near MRT stations, so beginners commuting from either side of the island have a studio within easy reach.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to speak Mandarin to join a Mandarin Yoga class?

No. Instructors teach bilingually, so instructions are given in both English and Mandarin during the same class.

Is the first class really free?

Yes, every new student at Desert Roses gets one free trial class with no obligation to continue.

What should I bring to my first class?

Comfortable clothing and a water bottle — mats are provided at both studio locations.
